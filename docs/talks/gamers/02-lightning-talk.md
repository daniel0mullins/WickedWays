# The Lightning Talk — for gamers (7–10 minutes)

**Title:** *One Game, Every Table*

**Audience:** gaming enthusiasts — tabletop players, board-game night
regulars, con-goers. No technical background assumed, and none used.
**Format:** 8 slides, ~1 minute each. Speaker notes are the script; say them
in your own words.

---

## Slide 1 — The game (1 min)

*Visual: the haunted house — a party marker at the front door, a candle-lit
title.*

Wicked Ways is a co-op horror RPG. Your party — with one player as the Game
Master — explores a haunted house room by room. Every character juggles
three stats: **Health**, **Sanity**, and **Energy**, and they protect each
other in a circle — your Sanity absorbs blows to your Health, your Energy
shields your Sanity, your Health carries your Energy. Let one collapse and
things get ugly: hit zero Sanity and you Panic; run out of Energy and you're
Confused, fumbling half your actions. The house is dark unless you carry
light, some monsters *prefer* the dark, and somewhere upstairs there's a way
out — if you can find the keys.

## Slide 2 — The problem with every digital board game (1 min)

*Visual: one game box splitting into four slightly-different copies: "the
app," "the tabletop version," "the tutorial," "the port."*

Here's a thing every gamer has felt: the digital version of a board game
never quite matches the table version. The app resolves an edge case one
way, the rulebook says another. The tutorial teaches a simplified fake. The
console port is its own thing entirely. Same box art — four slightly
different games, and you find the differences mid-argument, at the table.

Wicked Ways refuses that. There is exactly **one** set of rules — and every
way of playing is a window onto it.

## Slide 3 — One brain, every table (1 min)

*Visual: the game's "brain" in the center; spokes to browser, desktop app,
online multiplayer, physical board, and "next" (VR/voice).*

Every version of Wicked Ways — the browser game, the desktop app, playing
online with friends, even the physical board — is driven by the **same
rules brain**. Not copies of the rules. *The* rules, one place, and every
screen or board is just a window looking in. When you move, your move goes
to the brain; the brain resolves it and tells every window what happened.
That's why nothing ever plays differently: there's nothing else it could
play like.

## Slide 4 — Your dice are real (1.5 min)

*Visual: a d20 mid-roll; the outcome table: 20 crit ×1.5 · 1 stumble · 2–5
miss · 6–19 hit.*

Every attack starts with a **d20 to-hit** — roll a natural 20 and you crit
for half again the damage; roll a natural 1 and you *stumble*, hurting
yourself; 2 through 5 miss clean.

And on a physical table, you roll it **yourself**. Pick up your actual d20,
roll it, and the game accepts your number — for your attack, or for the
monster's. Don't feel like rolling? The house rolls for you. Either way,
the roll is written into the game's ledger, which is why the next slide is
even possible.

## Slide 5 — The game never lies (1.5 min)

*Visual: a ledger/quill; "every session, replayable to the last roll."*

Because everything — every move, every roll, every stumble — goes through
one brain and into one ledger, some things are simply *true* here that
digital games usually can't promise:

- **Saves never break.** Load a save and the world is exactly, precisely
  where you left it — mid-turn, mid-panic, mid-anything.
- **Every session can be replayed** to the last die roll. Great for
  settling arguments; even better for reporting bugs.
- **Nobody can cheat.** There's no host advantage, no edited client — every
  move from every player passes through the same referee, and the referee
  is the game itself.

## Slide 6 — Make your own nightmares (1.5 min)

*Visual: a campaign file opened like a grimoire — readable text: rooms,
doors, dialogue.*

A campaign is a **text file you can read**. Rooms and their descriptions.
Doors and which key opens them. Monsters, dialogue, victory conditions —
even a doom clock that ticks every round. If you can write a forum post,
you can write a haunted house.

Two promises. First: **we don't have a secret editor.** Hollow House — the
campaign that ships with the game — is written in exactly the format yours
would be. Whatever we can make the game do, you can. Second: **a broken mod
can't hurt you.** A campaign with an error in it refuses to load — it never
gets far enough to corrupt a save. The worst a bad mod can be is boring.

The ambition, said out loud: **as moddable as DOOM** — the game people are
still making levels for, thirty years on.

## Slide 7 — What's next (1 min)

*Visual: the board on a table; ghosted future windows around it: VR headset,
smart speaker, chat bubble, spectator screen.*

The physical board is the current frontier: e-ink tiles that repaint as the
party explores, pieces the board recognizes when you set them down (the
same tap-tech as a contactless card), little player dashboards, real dice —
and the whole thing is already playable as an on-screen simulator, because
the board is just another window onto the same brain.

And that's the fun of it — *anything* that can show you the house and take
your commands can be a table: a VR version where the house is around you, a
voice version you play by speaking, a Discord bot that holds a seat for
your group, a spectator view for streams. None of them change the game. The
game doesn't care what it's played on.

## Slide 8 — The takeaway (0.5–1 min)

*Visual: the four split copies from slide 2, healed into one box.*

One game. Not four almost-identical games — one, with many tables. Your
dice count, your saves are safe, your mods are welcome, and every table —
screen, board, or whatever comes next — is the same haunted house.

One game. Every table. Bring your own dice.

### Timing checkpoints

- End of slide 3: ~3.5 min. Long? Tighten slide 1 — the game intro should
  tease, not tutorialize.
- End of slide 6: ~7 min. The mod slide is the applause line for a con
  crowd; protect it.
- Slides 7–8 are 90 seconds of landing. If desperate, slide 7 compresses to
  the physical-board sentence alone.

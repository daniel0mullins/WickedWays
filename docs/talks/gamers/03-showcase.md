# The Showcase — for gamers (10–15 minutes)

**Title:** *Inside the Haunted House: a tour of Wicked Ways*

**Audience:** gaming enthusiasts who want the tour — what the game is, how
it plays, and what makes this one different. No technical background
assumed, and none used.
**Format:** 6 sections, ~2 minutes each. Timing checkpoints at the end.

---

## 0 — Frame (0.5 min)

One sentence of setup: Wicked Ways is a co-op horror RPG — a party and a
Game Master exploring a haunted house, turn by turn — and its one unusual
promise is that it is the *same game* on every table: browser, desktop,
online, or a real physical board. This talk tours the game first, then the
promise.

## 1 — The house (2 min)

*Visual: a generated map — rooms, doors, one locked, one dark.*

Every campaign is a house of connected rooms, and the game guarantees the
map always hangs together — every room reachable, no stranded corners.
Doors can be locked, and keys are treasures: the shipped campaign opens
with a trembling caretaker who hands you the cellar key and vanishes — and
that key is the only way down.

Darkness is a mechanic, not a mood. A dark room hides everything in it —
you can't attack, loot, or harvest what you can't see — until someone
brings a lantern or lights the room. But some monsters *see in the dark*
and *suffer in the light*: lighting a room both lets you aim and burns
them. Carrying the lantern or leaving it behind is a real decision, every
room.

## 2 — Your three lives (2 min)

*Visual: the Health / Sanity / Energy triangle, arrows in a circle.*

Characters have three stats, and they guard each other in a circle: Sanity
absorbs Health damage, Energy absorbs Sanity damage, Health absorbs Energy
damage. A rested guard stat shrugs a hit off entirely; an empty one
*doubles* it — so the house doesn't have to kill you, it just has to wear
you down until it can.

Fall too far and afflictions kick in and *stick*: zero Health is a
knock-out; zero Sanity is Panic — you can barely do anything but run; low
Sanity is Fear — you can do anything *except* run; zero Energy is
Confusion, where every action has a coin-flip chance of fizzling. Each
turn you get a shake-off roll that gets more generous the longer you
suffer. Horror games talk about dread; this one mechanizes the spiral.

## 3 — Dice night (2 min)

*Visual: the d20 outcome band: 1 stumble · 2–5 miss · 6–19 hit · 20 crit.*

Every attack — yours or the monster's — opens with a d20 to-hit: a natural
20 crits for half again the damage, a natural 1 stumbles into self-damage,
2–5 miss outright. Damage that lands gets filtered through the stat
triangle — armor first, then the guard stat.

Here's the signature move: at a physical table, **you roll the die**. Your
actual d20, your actual hand — and the game accepts the number, for your
attack or the monster's. Prefer not to? The house rolls. Either way the
roll is recorded in the game's ledger, so the session stays perfectly
replayable — your real dice don't break the game's memory, they *join* it.

## 4 — Playing together (2 min)

*Visual: four players around one glowing "brain"; one is on a phone
browser, one on a laptop, one at the physical board.*

Multiplayer here is not "everyone runs a copy and hopes." There is one
rules brain — think of it as the referee — and every player's move goes to
it. The referee resolves the move and tells everyone what happened. That
one idea quietly fixes the things that plague online board games:

- **No host advantage and no cheating** — nobody's device decides anything;
  the referee does.
- **No desyncs** — there's nothing to fall out of sync *with*; every screen
  is a window on the same game.
- **Turns are enforced** — you get your handful of actions, and when
  they're spent, they're spent; the referee won't take a fifth.
- **Saves and replays are exact** — the whole session is a ledger; load it
  or replay it and you get the same game, to the last roll.

## 5 — Make your own nightmares (2.5 min)

*Visual: a campaign text file beside the game it produces — a door line, a
dialogue line, a doom-clock line, each with an arrow into the world.*

A campaign is a readable text file: rooms, doors and their keys, monsters,
loot, NPC dialogue with fuzzy matching (ask the caretaker about "the
cellar" however you phrase it), victory conditions, even house-wide rules
like a doom clock that ticks each round or a dread that gnaws at whoever
walks without a lantern.

Three things to say plainly. **One:** the shipped campaign is written in
this exact format — we have no secret tools; whatever Hollow House does,
your campaign can do. **Two:** a broken campaign refuses to load with an
error — it cannot corrupt a save; the worst a bad mod can be is boring.
**Three:** a campaign you write runs on *every* table automatically —
browser, desktop, online, the physical board — you do nothing extra.

The ambition: **as moddable as DOOM.** The honest gap: easy *sharing* — a
one-file "here, run my campaign" hand-off — is still being built. The
format is done; the loading dock isn't.

## 6 — The physical board, and what's after it (2 min)

*Visual: the board exploded: e-ink tiles, NFC pieces, player dashboards,
dice tray — plus ghosted future tables: VR, voice, chat, spectators.*

The physical board: e-ink tiles that repaint as the party explores
(fog-of-war for real — unexplored rooms simply aren't drawn yet), pieces
the board recognizes when you set them down (the same tap-tech as a
contactless card), little per-player dashboards, and the dice tray from
section 3. The whole board already runs as an on-screen simulator, because
— say it together — the board is just another window onto the same brain.

And after it, anything that can show the house and take a command can be a
table: VR with the house around you, a voice version played by speaking, a
Discord seat for your group, spectator views for streams. The game doesn't
know what it's being played on. That's the whole trick.

## Close (0.5 min)

One haunted house. One set of rules. Every table — screen, board, headset,
or your kitchen table with real dice — is a window on the same game. Bring
your friends, bring your mods, bring your own d20.

- End of §2: ~5 min. Long? Compress §1's darkness beat to one sentence.
- End of §4: ~9 min. §4 is the differentiator for online players; protect

- §5 is the applause section for creators — never cut the "no secret
  tools" promise. §6 compresses to the board sentence + "anything can be a
  table" if you're at 13 min.
